# ryantornberg.com — spec

What this site is for, what belongs on it, and what does not. Written 2026-09-20, revised 2026-09-20 to drop long-form writing. Revisit when the job search ends.

## Purpose

One job: a hiring manager or recruiter who has your name spends 15 to 60 seconds and comes away convinced you think clearly at scale. Everything else is secondary.

Concretely, the site is the URL that goes in the Portfolio field of an application, in a LinkedIn message, and at the end of a cover letter. Its existence is worth more than its traffic.

## Audience, in priority order

1. A hiring manager for a principal, staff, architect or head-of-engineering role, skimming before a screen.
2. A recruiter checking that the résumé claims are real.
3. An engineer who followed a link from an application and wants to see whether the claims hold up.

Not the audience: search engines, other job seekers, or a general technical readership. No SEO work, no newsletter, no comments.

## The governing decision: artifacts, not essays

**This site carries no long-form writing.** Not now, and not later without revisiting this section.

Two reasons, and the second one is the durable one.

First, Ryan does not write blog posts. A site whose content depends on a writing habit he does not have is a site that rots. The maintenance section below used to plan for that rot; it is cheaper to remove the dependency.

Second, and more important: the site's job is to prove Ryan thinks clearly. Prose generated by an AI proves nothing about how Ryan thinks, no matter how good the prose is. The original version of this spec already said it — *"if it could have been written by someone who had not done the work, it does not go up"* — and the three essays that shipped in the first version failed that test. The sharpest version of the risk: an essay titled "Plausible is not checked" that was itself never checked is the single most quotable thing an interviewer could find. Removing it is not a retreat, it is the spec enforcing its own rule.

So the unit of proof is the **artifact**: something whose existence is the evidence, and which nobody asks who wrote the sentences of.

Qualifying artifacts, in order of strength:

1. **A public repository** that a reviewer can open and read. See "Planned" below.
2. **Diagrams** of mechanisms Ryan designed, with factual one-line captions.
3. **Facts with dates and numbers** — what was built, at what scale, verified how.

Not artifacts: essays, opinion posts, lessons-learned write-ups, industry commentary, a reading list, or a talks page with no talks. Each of these feels productive to produce and each one dilutes the parts that work.

## What it must do

- Say what Ryan does, in his own words, above the fold.
- Show proof a résumé cannot carry: real mechanisms, drawn.
- Give one obvious way to make contact.
- Load fast, read well on a phone, and work in light and dark themes.
- Survive neglect. With no writing to keep current, nothing on it should look stale in a year.

## What it must not do

- No employer-specific detail: no internal product names, ticket numbers, customer names, screenshots of internal tools, or unfixed security issues. The type of work is publishable; the specifics are not. (Career plan, settled decision 3.)
- No résumé PDF. It carries a phone number. Contact says "sent on request".
- No fabricated metrics. Every number traces to something checkable in Ryan's own records.
- No long-form writing, per the governing decision above.
- No analytics that track individuals, no chat widgets, no popups, no cookie banner (because nothing sets cookies).

## Structure

```
/                       the whole site: cover, what I do, selected work, background, contact
/SPEC.md                this file
/README.md              how to run and deploy
/DIAGRAMS.md            the diagram system: grid, type scale, figure conventions
```

One page. There is no `/writing` directory and no second page. If something does not fit on the index, the test is whether it is an artifact — and if it is, it belongs in the repository, not in a new page here.

Page sections, in order:

1. **Cover** — name, one-line positioning, the petrol field.
2. **What I do** — four short blocks: brownfield modernization, platform migrations, player-coach leadership, AI-assisted delivery. Three lines each, factual, no narrative.
3. **Selected work** — the four workspace diagrams, each with a one-line caption naming the mechanism. Framed as one exhibit among the work, not as the site's thesis.
4. **Background** — the span, compressed to four lines: municipal systems, defense research, healthcare, pharmacy. This is what keeps the site from reading as AI-only.
5. **Contact** — email, LinkedIn, GitHub. Résumé on request.

## Content rules

- **Every claim is checkable by Ryan.** Numbers come from his own logs, plans and commits. He must be able to answer a follow-up question about any line on the page in his own words, without preparation. That is the test each caption has to pass.
- **Captions are labels, not prose.** One line, stating what the mechanism is. If a caption needs a second sentence, the diagram is not carrying its weight.
- **Diagrams earn their place** by showing a mechanism prose would take a paragraph to describe. Hand-authored inline SVG, `currentColor` plus one accent, no libraries. Conventions in `DIAGRAMS.md`.
- **Plain language.** Short sentences, active voice, no jargon a competent engineering manager outside his stack would have to look up.
- **First person.** The site is him talking, not a company describing a person.

## Composition check

The site currently leans on AI-practice material, because that is where the diagrams come from. The career plan puts AI enablement *third* behind Principal/Staff Architect and hands-on Head of Engineering. Any addition should be judged on whether it moves the site toward or away from that balance. Adding a fifth workspace diagram makes the imbalance worse; the public repository and the Background section are what correct it.

## Planned

**`agent-ready-workspace`** — a public, clean-room repository: an `AGENTS.md` contract, a link and freshness checker, a symbol-lookup script, a definition-of-done template, and a sample eval. Written fresh on Ryan's own time and equipment, with no code copied from any employer repository.

This is the highest-leverage item available to the site. It turns the résumé's most distinctive claim into something a reviewer can open in 30 seconds, and it is the artifact that makes the diagrams stop carrying the page alone.

**Gate:** it goes public only after Ryan reads his employment agreement's invention-assignment clause. Some agreements claim work related to the employer's business even when done off-hours. Career plan §4.4 has the brief; settled decision 3 clears the type of work.

Nothing else is planned. That is deliberate.

## Design

- Cover: one petrol field (`#0e3a46`) with large condensed type as the image, in the spirit of Blue Note record sleeves. Reid Miles' covers ran to deep teal as readily as to blue; the earlier ultramarine read as a brand color rather than as ink. The rest of the page is quiet.
- The cover lifts to `#125463` in dark mode. At the light value it separates from a near-black page by only 1.51:1 and stops reading as a field; the lifted value gives 2.18:1 and still holds 7.8:1 behind the display type.
- One accent, derived from the cover: `#0f5568` on light, `#5cc2d6` on dark. Every pairing clears 4.5:1; most clear 7:1.
- Type: Archivo (condensed widths) for display, Source Serif 4 for body.
- Layout: left-aligned, 66-character measure for text, wider frame for diagrams.
- No motion. No hover effects beyond link underlines.
- Light and dark themes via `prefers-color-scheme`, both tested.
- Every figure has its own anchor, so a single diagram can be linked directly from a message or a cover letter.

## Technical

- Static HTML and CSS. No build step, no framework, no JavaScript.
- Hosted on GitHub Pages from `main` at `github.com/ryantornberg/ryantornberg.com`, custom domain via `CNAME`.
- DNS at Porkbun: four A records at GitHub Pages' addresses, `www` as a CNAME. Hand-managed; the Porkbun API keys do not exist.
- HTTPS is provisioned by GitHub. If the certificate is missing rather than pending, remove and re-add the custom domain to force a new request; this rewrites the `CNAME` file as a commit, so pull before editing.
- Fonts from Google Fonts with a real fallback stack; everything else self-contained.

## Maintenance

- Quarterly review, 15 minutes: is the positioning still right, does the résumé line still match, does the repository link still work.
- No content cadence. There is nothing to keep current by design.
- If two quarterly reviews pass with no edits and no applications in flight, take the site down or freeze it rather than let it rot.

## Success

There is no traffic target. The site works if:

1. It gets linked in every application and outreach message.
2. At least one interviewer mentions having looked at it.
3. Nothing on it ever has to be walked back.
